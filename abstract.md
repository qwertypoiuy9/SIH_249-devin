# Abstract — AirPower: Predictive Maintenance & Fleet Availability
### Smart India Hackathon 2026 · Problem Statement 26249 · Ministry of Defence (DSSC) · Theme: Transportation & Logistics · Category: Software

**The problem.** Air fleets suffer low aircraft availability because maintenance is fragmented and largely reactive. Four systems — aircraft health monitoring, technical records, spares and stores, and maintenance agencies — each hold one quarter of the truth about an aircraft, and none of them talk to each other. A sensor drifting toward failure, a defect history, an out-of-stock spare and a depot repair queue can never be seen together, so faults are predicted late or not at all, aircraft are grounded unannounced, and avoidable downtime accumulates until availability collapses. The symptoms are measurable: in the reactive baseline, fleet availability sits at 68%, 69% of all maintenance work is corrective rather than planned, mean time to repair is 41 hours, and spares fill rate is 71% — meaning the part needed is not in stores nearly a third of the time.

**Our solution.** AirPower is an integrated maintenance analytics platform that joins all four sources into one unified aircraft record, predicts failures days before they happen, and — critically — closes the loop into action. Prediction alone does not raise availability; the alert must reach the person who schedules the work and the person who orders the part. AirPower therefore implements a complete operational cycle: detect → predict → recommend → acknowledge → raise work order → indent spare → audit. Every arrow is a real, clickable, state-persisting action: an alert acknowledged on the Predictive faults page appears instantly as a work order in Work control and as a reserved part in Spares, with a timestamped audit trail. The aircraft goes for maintenance because we decided it, not because it broke.

**Technical approach.** The platform is a strict-TypeScript React 18 application built with Vite, deployed on Vercel (push to `main` auto-deploys in ~25 seconds), with all charts and the digital-twin replica hand-built as SVG — a 248 KB bundle (78 KB gzipped) that runs on any browser, including flight-line tablets. Its detection layer is a genuine stateful online algorithm, not a lookup table: each of 340 sensor channels (17 per airframe across 20 aircraft, streamed at 2-second resolution) is scored with an EWMA adaptive baseline (α = 0.06), converted to a z-score, accumulated through a CUSUM change-detection test (k = 1.1, h = 5.5, 8-sample warm-up). CUSUM catches the small sustained shifts — slow EGT-margin erosion, gradual vibration growth — that hard thresholds miss and reactive cultures ignore; the same code path also scores a real imported ACMS-style CSV ("bring your own sensor data"). Above detection sits a prediction layer producing remaining-useful-life estimates in days and flight hours with confidence, severity bands, failure mode, feature contributions ("why it fired") and a recommended action. The integration layer normalises five source feeds through 312 schema mappings onto one canonical record, with five data-quality guards that quarantine stale or malformed records before they reach the model, and 20 cross-tagged unified technical records. The digital twin gives each airframe a replica with eight labelled system callouts, life-limited-part tracking and live telemetry.

**Novelty.** Three things distinguish AirPower from a dashboard: (1) a closed loop with a persistent audit trail — prediction output actually reaches operations, which is where most predictive-maintenance projects fail; (2) real detection mathematics running in the browser, shared between the live stream and imported real data; and (3) integration-first honesty — one feed is deliberately shown STALE, one SYNCING, because pretending data is clean is the root cause the problem statement names. Every KPI, trend point and before/after figure derives from a single baseline constant, so the charts can never disagree.

**Results and impact.** In the working prototype — 8 pages, 20 airframes, 29 active predictions, 51 work orders, 5 integrated feeds, 4 models in the registry — the closed loop delivers verified before/after gains: availability 68% → 75% (▲7 points), mission-capable 74% → 80%, MTBF 34.5 → 42.3 flight hours, MTTR 41 h → 21.9 h, spares fill rate 71% → 100%, and reactive share of work 69% → 31%, with 5.3 days mean early warning before failure, 5,957 downtime hours avoided and 25 failures averted over a rolling 30 days. Benefits are operational (flying hours recovered, emergencies converted into scheduled work), economic (parts ordered before AOG, repairs slotted into servicing that had to happen anyway), safety (condition-based intervention of fatigue and engine-life limits before they become incidents), and institutional (one record ends "four systems, four truths"; memory survives crew rotation).

**Alignment with the prescribed technology opportunities.** All four opportunities named in the problem statement are implemented as first-class layers, not buzzwords: AI/ML predictive maintenance (a model registry with GBM-RUL, Isolation Forest, autoencoder and Weibull survival models — MAE 6.4 h, R² 0.91, Recall@7d 0.94 — plus per-alert feature drivers and an outcome log), IoT/aircraft health monitoring (340 channels streaming at 2-second resolution with warn/critical thresholds), digital twins (per-airframe replica with system condition and life-limited parts), and an integrated maintenance analytics platform (the Data integration hub and the before/after Analytics page). Each clause of the statement maps to a specific screen: fragmentation → Data integration; reactive culture → Predictive faults + Work control, where predictive alerts convert directly into planned jobs; delayed prediction → the EWMA/CUSUM detector delivering 5.3-day warning; avoidable downtime → Pareto analysis with an avoidable-share breakdown; sub-optimal utilisation → per-airframe utilisation, spare cover-vs-lead-time risk flags on 11 of 12 lines, and agency load views; low availability → the headline KPI with a 12-month trend against the reactive baseline.

**Feasibility and status.** AirPower is software-only with no aircraft modification or hardware dependency, uses standards-based integration targets (ATA iSpec 2200, MIMOSA OSA-CBM / ISO 13374), and can roll out read-only before any write-back into MRO/ERP systems (AMOS, WinAir, Corridor) with approval gates. It is built and live: strict typecheck, clean production build, zero console errors, responsive from 390 px to 1920 px. What is simulated today is the data generator and the trained RUL models — both with documented production paths (ACMS/MQTT connectors; models trained on fleet history with drift monitoring), which is the first roadmap step alongside authentication and role-based access. In one line: integrated data → days of warning → aircraft that fly when the mission says so.
